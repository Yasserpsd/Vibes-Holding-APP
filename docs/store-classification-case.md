## Letter (Apple version)

Subject: Classification inquiry — annual club membership that primarily covers in-person services (App: Investors Club, Apple ID 6825963092)

Dear App Review team,

We operate Investors Club, a business club with a physical headquarters in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia (app: "Investors Club", Apple ID 6825963092, account holder VIBES VENTURES COMPANY HOLDING). We currently sell our annual membership in the app exclusively through In-App Purchase, in full compliance with guideline 3.1.1, and we will remain fully compliant whatever the outcome of this inquiry.

We are asking for your guidance on the correct classification of this membership, because its substance is overwhelmingly a real-world service consumed outside the app:

- **A licensed physical venue.** The club is run from a 347 m² headquarters in Olaya, Riyadh, leased under an official Ejar contract (SAR 270,000 per year plus VAT; SAR 621,000 paid since December 2024). The tenant, our subsidiary Positive Vibes for Business Incubators and Accelerators, holds Monsha'at (Small and Medium Enterprises General Authority) licence no. 1498 as a business incubator, accelerator and coworking space. Active members book and use the meeting rooms and workspace at no extra charge.
- **In-person events.** Throughout the year the club holds meetups, evening gatherings and workshops. The club pays hotel venues and full catering for each gathering, and pays external facilitators who deliver the workshops. Admission to all of these is included in the membership at no extra charge.
- **Staffed on site.** A full-time team runs the headquarters, member hospitality and the events.
- **Economic substance.** The membership fee (SAR 1,900 per year) funds this physical hospitality: rent, venues, catering, facilitators and staff make up the overwhelming majority of what it pays for.
- **The app is a companion.** Its features support the physical experience: booking HQ rooms, the events agenda, contacting the club's administration, and browsing partnership opportunities whose purpose is to lead to in-person meetings.

Guideline 3.1.3(e) provides that apps may use purchase methods other than in-app purchase for goods and services that will be consumed outside of the app. Given the facts above, we would like to ask:

1. Does our annual membership qualify, in whole or in part, as a service consumed outside of the app under 3.1.3(e)?
2. If not as currently structured, is there a structure you would consider compliant — for example, a membership covering only the physical services (venue access, meetups, workshops, hospitality) sold outside of IAP, with the app's digital features provided to members at no charge?

We are ready to share supporting documents on request: our commercial registrations and ownership records, the Monsha'at incubator licence, the Ejar lease and rent invoices, invoices for hotel venues, catering and workshop facilitators, our events calendar with photos, and a short video of the headquarters.

We appreciate the App Store and simply want to classify this correctly. Thank you for your time and guidance.

## Letter (Google version)

Subject: Payments policy scope question — club membership that primarily covers in-person services (com.vcmem.investorsclub)

Hello Google Play Developer Support,

We publish Investors Club (com.vcmem.investorsclub), the app of a business club with a physical headquarters in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. Our annual membership is currently sold in the app through Google Play Billing, in full compliance with the Payments policy, and we will remain compliant whatever the outcome of this question.

Google Play's Payments policy requires Play Billing for digital goods and services, and states that it must not be used for physical goods and services. We would like your guidance on which category our membership falls into, because its substance is overwhelmingly physical:

- A 347 m² headquarters in Olaya, Riyadh under an official Ejar lease (SAR 270,000 per year plus VAT), operated by our subsidiary under Monsha'at licence no. 1498 as a business incubator, accelerator and coworking space. Members use its meeting rooms and workspace at no extra charge.
- Regular in-person meetups and workshops, for which the club pays hotel venues, full catering and external facilitators. Admission is included in the membership.
- A full-time on-site team running the headquarters and the events.
- The app supports this physical experience: room booking, the events agenda, contacting the administration, and partnership opportunities that lead to in-person meetings.

Our questions:

1. Is the annual membership, whose economic substance is venue access, physical hospitality and in-person events, a physical service for which an alternative payment flow (our licensed local processor, Moyasar) is required or permitted in-app?
2. If the current membership is treated as digital, would a membership restructured to cover only the physical services, with the app's digital features provided at no charge, be classified as physical?

Supporting documents are attached: commercial registrations and ownership records, the Monsha'at licence, the Ejar lease and rent invoices, event venue, catering and facilitator invoices, our events calendar with photos, and a short video of the headquarters.

## Negotiation notes (internal)

- Before sending: confirm the membership price (SAR 1,900) and that the facilities named (meeting rooms, workspace) match the 347 m² unit; add any second venue lease to the rent figures. Every number must match a document.
- The stores judge what the member buys, not how costly the business is. Lead with physical consumption; costs only serve as proof of it.
- Weak point: the membership also unlocks in-app digital features (Projects Bank unlocks, advisor, contact-admin). Both stores may rule "hybrid = digital". The fallback in question 2 (physical-only membership + free digital companions) is the strongest realistic outcome — decide with the owner before conceding it.
- Never mention or imply steering users to external payment from inside the app; rule 3 stays in force regardless.
- If a store declines: the status quo already costs only 15% (Small Business Program / Play subscriptions) on store-acquired buyers; external channels remain 100%. Do not escalate into a dispute that could affect the account.
